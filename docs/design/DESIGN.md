## 1. The experience

The learner picks a scene, talks to a character, and gets coached from the
sideline without ever being interrupted.

Three things happen at once, and the design is mostly the discipline of keeping
them apart:

| | What it is | What it must never do |
|---|---|---|
| **The character** | The person you're talking to | Correct you, or break character |
| **The coach** | Feedback on what you just said | Speak, or interrupt the scene |
| **The memory** | What you struggled with last time | Read out a transcript |

That separation is what lets the product promise *"never lectured mid-scene"* —
the character is never told what you got wrong, so it has nothing to bring up.

---

## 2. The three constraints that drive everything

`PRODUCT.md` lists eight "never" rules. Three of them shape the whole interface:

**Never ambiguous silence.** Voice has no cursor. The learner cannot tell
"thinking" from "broken", so the app has to say which — visibly, in more than one
way at once.

**Never lectured mid-scene.** Feedback is *pull-shaped*, not push-shaped: cards
land in a rail the learner may never read, and the coach never speaks.
Corrections are capped so the rail can't become a wall of red.

**Never a dead end.** Mic failure, a silent learner, and a mid-sentence quit all
have a defined, non-punitive path. Nothing in the product can leave the learner
without a next action.

## 3. The loop

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │  scene card  →  goal + who + what's in the way           │
   └───────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                               ▼
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  TURN LOOP                                   │
        │                                              │
        │   the character speaks first                 │
        │        ↓          (never a blank page)       │
        │   the learner talks (or types)               │
        │        ↓                                     │
        │   interrupted? the character stops talking,  │
        │   and nothing already said is taken back     │
        │        ↓                                     │
        │   the character replies                      │
        │   the coach quietly writes a card            │
        └──────────────┬───────────────────────────────┘
                       ▼
              End (or quit, or drop)
                       ▼
        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
        │  DEBRIEF — always produced, never skipped    │
        │  did you win · what worked · what to watch   │
        │  · every sentence, corrected · next scene    │
        └──────────────────┬───────────────────────────┘
                           ▼
        next session opens: "Last time you dropped past tense
        three times — let's see if it sticks."
```

My progress sits beside the loop rather than in it: it is where a learner looks
*between* sessions, when the question is no longer "what do I say next" but "is
this working". It reads only what the loop already produced — goal outcomes,
attempt counts, and the coach's cards — so it needs no new instrumentation, and
it always ends by pushing the learner back into an unfinished scene. It is also
the only place the product shows a number, and §4 is where the weighting that
makes that safe is argued.

---

## 4. Decisions worth arguing about

**Interruption is a first-class feature, not a bug.** Barge-in is on the *never
cut* list, so it is designed for: interrupting stops the character mid-sentence,
and nothing already said is taken back.

**No pronunciation score, and no judgement of the learner.** `PRODUCT.md` rules a
pronunciation score out because it is the least actionable number available, and
grades the person rather than the attempt. The debrief reports counts and quotes
instead — "three times", "you interrupted once and it landed".

**Progress is one number, weighted so it cannot contradict the badge beside it.**
`PRODUCT.md` asks for a per-goal "score" built from time on task, suggestions
adopted, and nice/sharpened feedbacks, plus multi-attempt tracking. That is a
composite, and a composite is exactly what the rule above would normally refuse —
so the *weights* are where the rule survives rather than in the absence of a
number. Meeting the goal is worth 55 of the 100 and the three engagement
components share the other 45, which means a win can never score below 55 and a
miss can never score above 45. The score therefore cannot contradict the *Goal
met* badge printed next to it, and no amount of talking can make a failure
outrank a success. An even split would break exactly that: a long, talkative miss
would beat a short win, and a number that lies is worse than no number.

Everything else the score is allowed to be follows from the same test — *can this
mislead?* It is a ring, not a grade: no A/B/C, no stars, no "needs work". It is
one attempt's number, never a delta, so the screen never says "worse than last
time". An unstarted scene shows no ring at all, because a 0 reads as a failure
the learner never had. And the weighting is printed where the number appears,
because a number a learner cannot decode is a number they will misread.

The same test still bans the obvious affordances: no progress bar, no streak, no
percentage of a goal, no ranking against other learners. And *shape* still does
work the number cannot — the attempt-by-attempt breakdown shows more suggestions
used and more upgrades earned before the goal flips, which is what "am I
improving" actually looks like.

**Feedback is written twice.** Every correction carries an English line (what to
say) and a Chinese line (why), because a correction the learner can't parse
teaches nothing. Chinese is a per-user toggle for learners who outgrow it.

**Typing is a peer mode, not a fallback.** It shares the transcript, the coach,
and the debrief; only the input surface changes. That keeps "the mic died" from
becoming a different, degraded product.

**Memory stores patterns, not transcripts.** The next session needs *"past tense,
three times"*, not yesterday's audio — which keeps the callback specific and the
learner's most sensitive data unkept.

**The scene ends on its own.** A scene that only ends when you press End is a
scene you abandon. The character converges on a natural close once the goal is
met or the conversation has run its course — in character, with no countdown.
